# Abundance and Biomass of Sand Prawn *Kraussillichirus kraussi* in Richards Bay Harbour

## Overview
This project investigates the abundance and biomass of the sand prawn *Kraussillichirus kraussi* in Richards Bay Harbour, South Africa. *K. kraussi* is an ecologically important macrobenthic species, contributing to sediment bioturbation and serving as prey for higher trophic levels. Understanding its population dynamics provides insights into estuarine ecosystem functioning and environmental health.

## Objectives
- Quantify the abundance and biomass of *K. kraussi* across multiple sites in Richards Bay Harbour.
- Explore the influence of sediment grain size and total organic content (TOC) on prawn distribution.
- Provide baseline data to support estuarine management and conservation strategies.

## Methodology
- **Field Sampling:** Sediment samples collected using quadrats and corers at different sites.
- **Measurements:** Sediment grain size, total organic content (TOC), and prawn abundance and biomass recorded.
- **Data Analysis:** Statistical analyses performed to identify spatial patterns and relationships between environmental factors and prawn populations.

## Repository Contents
- `data/` – Raw and processed datasets.
- `scripts/` – Python scripts used for data analysis and visualization.
- `figures/` – Graphs and visualizations of abundance and biomass patterns.
- `report/` – Final report summarizing methodology, results, and discussion.

## Significance
This repository provides a reproducible resource for researchers, environmental managers, and students interested in estuarine ecology, macrobenthic communities, and the ecological role of sand prawns. The data and analysis workflows can support future studies on estuarine macrobenthos in South Africa and similar environments worldwide.
